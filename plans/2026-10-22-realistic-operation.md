# Realistic mining operation: staged delivery

The persistent goal is a direct-control placer gold-mining operation as realistic as possible, using the Gold Rush television operation as the reference. It includes real overburden/pay excavation and transport, configurable and upgradeable trommel/shaker wash plants, sluice loading/clogging and gold washout, pads, mud, constructed settling ponds, recirculation, tailings, cleanup and high quality graphics. The goal remains incomplete until the complete operation works and is verified. The water-rendering checkpoint `71a195b` is a starting point for these stages, not the completion criterion.

### Site and equipment failure chains

Simon added on 27 October that the operation must also emulate **wetness or overflow breaching a settling-pond berm, tailings accumulating too high, shaker/trommel malfunctions, and floods washing away bridges**. These remain part of the full goal alongside the preceding table.

- Berms and ponds: retain constructed material, geometry, saturation and hydraulic state. Model overtopping erosion and loss of support/strength under wet conditions; qualify seepage/internal erosion separately. A breach must release the actual stored water and sediment, alter downstream flow and remain repairable with finite placed material. Existing dry heightfield erosion alone is not a qualified berm-failure model.
- Tailings: actual deposits change elevation, storage and outlet clearance. High piles can obstruct discharge/access, back water up or become unstable; saturation, slumping and erosion need qualification. A growing decorative mesh or an arbitrary fullness meter does not establish this behaviour.
- Shaker and trommel: persistent component condition, feed/oversize accumulation, blocked passages and actual drive loads must support jams, screen damage, drive/bearing failures, shutdown and repair. Preserve all material/water during failures and restarts. Distinguish modeled overload/obstruction from explicitly parameterized reliability assumptions; do not claim calibrated wear or fault rates without evidence.
- Floods and bridges: changing upstream flow and terrain must produce the flood, with finite water budgets. Bridges need placed geometry, supports/foundations and structural state before flood loads, debris blockage and scour can undermine or wash them away. Displaced structure/debris, released loads and redirected flow remain owned and saved; these systems are currently absent.

Simon further described the target as literally all the operational drama from Gold Rush, explicitly adding **paydirt inaccessible because ground is too wet, varying pay volumes/grades revealed by surveys, getting bogged, and collapses from digging too deeply**. Interpret the reference across geology/prospecting, weather/water, earthwork/access, equipment, infrastructure, waste and recovery; the following named cases are requirements, not an exhaustive completion of that operational reference.

- Deposit and surveys: store spatially varying layer depth/thickness, grade, particle distribution and ground conditions independently of player knowledge. Surveys, drilling/test pits and assays sample that actual finite deposit and reveal estimates with sampling limits; surveys must not regenerate or improve ore. Preserve sample material/gold, holes, survey records and uncertainty across saves. Changing access, costs and surveyed pay volume/grade must affect the operation.
- Inaccessible wet pay: retained soil/groundwater, saturation, inflow, drainage and pump capacity can prevent workable access to the real pay layer. Dewatering, diversion/drains, staging or finite ground improvements must address the cause while preserving water budgets. Existing surface wetness and a dry digging model are insufficient evidence.
- Wet ground and feed: moisture-dependent soil strength/flow must support ground slumping and material-specific cohesive/sticky bulk behaviour. Overly wet pay can bridge or plug hoppers/chutes and blind screens. Retain solid grading, clay/fines, gold and pore water through blockage, drainage, operator clearing, shutdown and restart. Moisture is not assumed to affect every material monotonically in the same way; qualify the actual material/moisture/geometry range. Existing wet colour and free-flowing bulk inventories do not establish these behaviours.
- Bogging and recovery: load, soil bearing/strength, saturation, track support, slip, sinkage and ruts can immobilize or strand machines. Pads, route choice, unloading, drainage and physically supported recovery equipment must alter those conditions. Save the machine, soil deformation and load through the incident and recovery.
- Deep-cut collapse: actual cut geometry, removed support, soil properties and water state determine instability. Failed soil/clods, gold and water move into new owned positions and can bury equipment or block drainage/access. Benign shallow/stable cuts must remain stable under the same model. Heightfield depth limits and a visual landslide alone are not qualified excavation-collapse mechanics.

These incidents must join the same persistent operation: for example uncertain wet pay leads to dewatering and haul-road work, which affects pond capacity and discharge, while a storm or blocked plant changes the operating constraints again. Operator choices, repair/recovery, time and resource costs belong to the loop. Broader weather/seasonal, access/logistics, power/fuel and component-reliability scenarios remain to be specified and integrated under the full operational reference. Keep uncertainty and uncalibrated material/fault assumptions explicit.

Acceptance requires reproducible cause-to-effect scenarios, interventions that change those causes, preserved water/material/gold and structural inventory, and save/reload through failure and repair. Include benign controls and changed flow/feed/soil/geometry cases. Do not call these complete from a visual destruction sequence. They extend the complete-operation goal; the first integrated shift remains the next milestone.

Model qualification references for this expanded scope: [USACE EM 1110-2-1902, Slope Stability (2003)](https://www.publications.usace.army.mil/Portals/76/Publications/EngineerManuals/EM_1110-2-1902.pdf) provides the slope-analysis reference; [FHWA bridge-scour guidance and HEC-18](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/engineering/hydraulics/scourtech/scour.cfm) anchors the bridge/foundation cases. These sources guide future verification cases; they do not calibrate the current game or prove these failure systems implemented.
