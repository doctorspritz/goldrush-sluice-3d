Coarse environmental gold is a passive provenance tag of the paydirt class. It now follows residence-weighted deposition during actual water substeps, then the separate scour stage. Global tag conservation alone does not establish correct local custody; the earlier [marked-pulse regression](../playtests/2026-10-22-local-gold.md) exposed that distinction before the residence correction.

The recorder reads old water depth, the limited shared X/Z face volumes and boundary discharge at each actual water substep. Two later records capture paydirt geometric thickness, counted precision stock, concentration and water depth before and after the final scour stage. A separate read-only compute pass packs each record. It never writes a physical water, sediment or geology buffer. The recorder itself changes no physical equation. Water CFL count, timestep/grid policy, FLIP/APIC and DEM remain. The recorder adds no intermediate CPU/GPU synchronization: all events return through the existing terrain readback.

The host now follows [residence-weighted material transport](residence-transport.md) in f64. Each substep partitions a donor tag between retained mobile material, departure and local deposition. Incoming parcels split between recipient mobile and bed tags using their remaining residence time. New arrivals can travel farther on the next substep. Final face routes receive each donor's rounding remainder, and exports remain explicit.

The two later records now bracket scour after the integrated transport/deposition. Gold already deposited during those substeps is not deposited again. Scour receives the bed gold fraction `removed_bed / previous_bed`. High and low bed components are differenced separately, with unchanged host precision residue cancelled before subtraction. This remains passive provenance for the implemented coarse paydirt law, not independent environmental gold settling.

Gold attached to mobile precision stock remains local while that stock is outside represented concentration. Each terrain upload splits the well-mixed mobile tag between represented and precision carriers, and joins surviving tags after transport/deposition and scour. When projection leaves no actual mobile paydirt carrier, its remaining tag strands into the bed. This also gives legacy unattached tags finite custody; it cannot reconstruct a historically incorrect location. [Precision stock](subgrid-bed.md) and its unresolved sub-ULP transport remain explicit approximations.

The current collapse law moves sediment, overburden and gravel; it leaves the paydirt stratum stationary. A real production control confirms that a non-pay heap can collapse while its underlying paydirt gold remains stationary. This is an audit of the current layered model, not a claim that all gold-bearing soil failure is resolved. Arbitrary fill stratigraphy, moving paydirt failure blocks and independently transported gold grains in the environment remain part of local-soil work. Density separation within the sluice remains the actual FLIP/DEM calculation.

Event storage costs 16 bytes per cell per recorded event: `water_substeps + 2` events. At 128×128 with two water substeps this is 1 MiB of GPU history plus readback; the existing 64-substep limit bounds it at 16.5 MiB. The capture shader uses seven read-only physical bindings and one history binding. Event state is transient. Version 5 saves keep the existing authoritative f64 bed/mobile/export tags, and restore rebuilds events on the next actual terrain step.

Controls include marked paydirt in all four directions, two-step routing into a second neighbour, open export, sequential deposition/scour with unchanged net bed height, tiny precision-stock deposition, mixed-provenance live restore and non-pay collapse. A real-GPU comparison checks recorded depth/flux budgets and bit-identical physical channels with tracing on and off for both closed and open boundaries. These numerical controls do not calibrate ore grades, gold recovery, dense slurries or constructed settling-pool behaviour. Continuous sloping shoreline drying/rewetting and under-1-mm rough-bed scour remain open.
