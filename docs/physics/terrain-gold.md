Coarse environmental gold is a passive provenance tag of the paydirt class. The terrain tracer now replays the actual water substeps, then deposition, then scour. The previous implementation exchanged the net final bed change first and advected once using cumulative fluxes. It conserved the total tag while leaving it in the wrong cell or phase: a marked 0.8 mm film delivered paydirt equivalent to 0.000220485 g of gold to its neighbour, but only about 7e-13 g of gold arrived.

The recorder reads old water depth, the limited shared X/Z face volumes and boundary discharge at each actual water substep. Two later records capture paydirt geometric thickness, counted precision stock, concentration and water depth after each bed stage. A separate read-only compute pass packs each record. It never writes a physical water, sediment or geology buffer. The original solver equations, CFL count, timestep, grid, FLIP/APIC and DEM remain. The recorder adds no intermediate CPU/GPU synchronization: all events return through the existing terrain readback.

The host advects f64 gold with the same upwind donor-water fractions, `q / (old_depth * cell_area)`. Every substep reads a snapshot of its donor tags, so arrivals can travel farther on the following substep. Each donor's removed budget is split among actual outgoing faces and boundary export, with the final route receiving the rounding remainder. No cell can spend more than its donor tag.

The two bed phases then operate separately. A deposit receives the active mobile gold fraction `delta_bed / (delta_bed + remaining_C * depth)`. Scour receives the bed gold fraction `removed_bed / previous_bed`. Sequential settlement and scour can mix provenance even when their net bed change is zero. Bed high and low components are differenced separately before summation, preserving tiny counted deposits that would vanish when first added to a large f64 geometric height. This tracer follows the implemented [mixed-column deposition and scour model](terrain-settling.md); it is not an independent gold settling law.

Gold attached to mobile precision stock remains local while that stock is outside represented concentration. Each terrain upload splits the well-mixed mobile tag between represented and precision carriers, and joins the surviving tags after the two bed stages. When projection leaves no actual mobile paydirt carrier, its remaining tag strands into the bed. This also gives legacy unattached tags finite custody; it cannot reconstruct a historically incorrect location. [Precision stock](subgrid-bed.md) and its unresolved sub-ULP transport remain explicit approximations.

The current collapse law moves sediment, overburden and gravel; it leaves the paydirt stratum stationary. A real production control confirms that a non-pay heap can collapse while its underlying paydirt gold remains stationary. This is an audit of the current layered model, not a claim that all gold-bearing soil failure is resolved. Arbitrary fill stratigraphy, moving paydirt failure blocks and independently transported gold grains in the environment remain part of local-soil work. Density separation within the sluice remains the actual FLIP/DEM calculation.

Event storage costs 16 bytes per cell per recorded event: `water_substeps + 2` events. At 128×128 with two water substeps this is 1 MiB of GPU history plus readback; the existing 64-substep limit bounds it at 16.5 MiB. The capture shader uses seven read-only physical bindings and one history binding. Event state is transient. Version 5 saves keep the existing authoritative f64 bed/mobile/export tags, and restore rebuilds events on the next actual terrain step.

Controls include marked paydirt in all four directions, two-step routing into a second neighbour, open export, sequential deposition/scour with unchanged net bed height, tiny precision-stock deposition, mixed-provenance live restore and non-pay collapse. A real-GPU comparison checks recorded depth/flux budgets and bit-identical physical channels with tracing on and off for both closed and open boundaries. These numerical controls do not calibrate ore grades, gold recovery, dense slurries or constructed settling-pool behaviour. Continuous sloping shoreline drying/rewetting and under-1-mm rough-bed scour remain open.
