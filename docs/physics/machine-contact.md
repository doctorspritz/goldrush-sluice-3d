The optional [articulated mechanism validation](articulated-machine.md) adds dynamic links and bounded actuator energy. It is explicitly selected with `--articulated-machine`; ordinary playable shifts still use the cutting-capable model described below. Validation-mode cutting/dumping remain unavailable pending soil/custody coupling.

The excavator now has a dynamic rigid chassis with gravity, oriented terrain contacts and differential track traction. Its boom, stick and bucket are prescribed, force/power-limited links held within each mechanical step. This is a bounded machine-contact foundation, not a calibrated excavator, deformable-soil or full hydraulic model. It leaves the original FLIP/APIC, riffle, DEM and terrain-water equations unchanged.

The pinned `rapier3d = 0.36.1` dependency uses `enhanced-determinism` without its parallel feature. It adds no physics thread pool. Machine, joint and finite-cut updates share a default 240 Hz mechanical clock; public steps subdivide without changing elapsed physical time. Rapier uses four TGS integration stages and sixteen internal PGS constraint iterations, Coulomb point friction, clustered contacts, no contact recycling and 0.1 mm allowed linear contact error. TGS stages and PGS convergence iterations have different roles: increasing the former alone failed the retained drive-refinement control. Gyroscopic forces are enabled by the pinned builder's default.

The local terrain is a 65×65 heightfield of actual site elevations at the existing 25 cm physical spacing. Its 16 m square origin moves in 4 m increments. Column-major storage maps rows to Z and columns to X. `FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES` prevents coplanar triangle seams from becoming physical track bumps. An immutable shape cache is reused only when every sampled height and the origin match. Fixed claim walls and Session-supplied hopper/sluice boxes supply finite collisions; these equipment boxes remain geometric approximations. Removing support changes the contacts and allows the body to fall or tip instead of snapping its origin to the highest of four terrain samples.

The rigid mass model contains two 1,300 kg tracks, a 700 kg undercarriage, a 3,000 kg upper house, a 1,300 kg boom, an 840 kg stick and a 250 kg empty bucket. Actual carried wet mass is added at the bucket floor. Collider geometry supplies the composite centre of mass and inertia. The cab supplies collision geometry with zero additional mass. The sloped bucket-floor collider follows the actual heel-to-tooth floor and lies above that cutting surface; an earlier box below it incorrectly lifted the machine during legitimate cuts. Side walls, curved bucket contact and actual granular payload distribution remain incomplete. The rigid surface triangles can jack the body during a cut rather than resolve deformable cutting soil. A declared 4 s joystick scan yields 10.06 L for boom-only motion and 24.68 L for a coordinated boom/crowd/curl stroke with 16.4° body tilt. This is a demonstrated applicability limit, tracked in `sluice-bucket-soil-contact`; it is not calibrated digging or a closed soil/contact reaction model.

These dimensions and the 8,440 kg base mass are an 8-ton-class reference: the [Cat 308 CR specification, AEHQ8163-06](https://s7d2.scene7.com/is/content/Caterpillar/CM20181002-32771-62524), pages 2–3, lists minimum operating mass without bucket, 450 mm shoes, 2,880 mm overall undercarriage length, 2,300 mm overall width and 5.1 km/h high travel speed. The modeled rectangular track footprint uses that overall length, not a measured contact patch. Component mass splits, the 250 kg bucket, cab/boom shapes and the dry-ground friction coefficient 0.65 are declared assumptions. They do not establish measured ground pressure, gradeability, bearing strength or wet traction.

Each belt requests tangential contact velocity, projected into the actual contact plane. Straight travel is capped at 5.1/3.6 m/s; steering adds opposite 0.42 m/s belt offsets. The solver bounds tangential impulse by `|Jt| ≤ μ Jn`. Zero friction and airborne tracks therefore cannot drive or steer the body. Released controls request zero belt speed, giving an ideal contact brake. This is an ideal speed-controlled motor limited by contact friction; engine torque, gearing, traction-power limits, rolling resistance, deformable belts and service-brake pressure are not modeled.

Feedback reports actual contact count, normal reaction, bucket normal reaction vector, peak tangential/normal impulse ratio, mass, kinetic energy, centre of mass and tilt. Body dynamics advance before joint acceptance, so rejecting a joint stroke never rolls back chassis gravity or contact movement. The existing finite swept cut and bedrock/capacity/force checks remain. Prescribed joint work now includes positive link/payload gravitational lift and work against the preceding bucket normal reaction, in addition to the existing soil-resistance estimate:

`W = Fsoil × floor_travel + max(ΔUlinks, 0) + max(−Rbucket · Δxfloor, 0)`

Accepted motion must fit the existing actuator force envelope and `W ≤ Ptool Δt`. This supplies a useful normal-load constraint, including a bucket supporting the chassis. It is not a complete actuator wrench, tangential bucket reaction, hydraulic circuit or proof of closed machine energy/momentum. Prescribed joint changes still change mass geometry without solving articulated recoil; payload/spoil exchange and dumping retain their previous finite-transfer approximation.

All mounted visual parts, the tool sweep and cab camera now use the body's full quaternion. Swing is relative to the tilted chassis; the vertical floor intersection uses its actual two triangles. Machine dynamics continue with the engine off and while the player is on foot. The operating replay first raises a closed loaded bucket, carries it back to the receiving pad using track controls, and aims the actual dump mouth from the tilted chassis frame. It returns to the advancing face and targets at least 0.4 m³ delivered, with a finite twelve-load guard. A fixed four-load assumption and fixed transport angles failed retained native/CPU controls. Hopper acceptance geometry and the minimum delivery gate are unchanged.

Ordinary save version 6 carries chassis tilt, centre-of-mass velocity, angular velocity, friction and mechanical cadence. Earlier binaries reject it. Versions 2–5 migrate absent track state to an initially stationary, upright body while retaining their inventories and terrain clock/moisture/precision-stock migrations. A legacy-version file containing moving new dynamics is rejected. Contact worlds are rebuilt at every mechanical boundary: no hidden warm-start impulse is required for exact continuation. The immutable terrain cache is deliberately absent from saves. Articulated validation writes version 7; current loaders accept versions 2–7 and require v7 exactly when the complete articulated state is present.

[The track-contact playtest report](../playtests/2026-10-23-track-contact.md) records the completed checks and outstanding gates. Macro 30/60/120 Hz agreement with the same internal clock is batch invariance; the separate 120/240/480 Hz test is physical mechanical-step refinement. Neither demonstrates convergence of the 25 cm soil geometry, full hydraulics or calibrated recovery. Stages 2–6 of the [operation plan](../../plans/2026-10-22-realistic-operation.md) remain open.
