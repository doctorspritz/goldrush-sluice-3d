Terrain erosion and settling use a snapshot, store and reconciliation protocol. This corrects a neighbour-read race and a mismatch between requested transfers and stored terrain changes. It preserves the existing hydraulic and deposition parameters; it does not establish a calibrated settling-pool model.

Each bed stage runs three ordered GPU passes:

1. Snapshot the four old material layers and compute slope from unchanged neighbouring geology. World edges use one-sided differences. A tile may read unchanged neighbours outside its own boundary.
2. Evaluate the existing settling or exposed-layer erosion law and store the candidate bed change. This pass does not change suspended concentration.
3. Read the actual stored layer delta and transfer that amount between bed and suspension. Reject an exchange if the recipient cannot represent any change or its donor would become negative. Surface tags, disturbance timers and debug counters follow accepted changes.

A separate pass for step 3 matters: computing `(old + request) - old` within the writer can be reassociated to `request` by GPU floating-point optimisation. The storage boundary gives reconciliation the same bed value that readback and the material inventory observe. Concentration still rounds to f32; this is measured finite-precision conservation, not an exact-arithmetic guarantee.

The existing four concentration transport targets temporarily hold the four old layer arrays. Interleaved auxiliary slot 7 holds the current stage's slope. Water donor, face-flux, boundary-water and exported-material slots retain their meanings. These aliases are derived scratch and add no serialized fields or storage buffers. Erosion snapshots again after deposition, so it sees the updated geometry. Complete simulation steps restore concentration targets once after both stages; isolated `dispatch_bed_exchange_tile` calls restore them before returning.

Water transport writes only its active tile but copies whole target arrays. It now initializes those targets from current concentrations before the water loop. This preserves CPU edits and inactive cells when tiles move, and prevents scratch layer heights from becoming suspended material.

Nine opt-in real-GPU controls in `crates/mining-physics/tests/terrain_exchange.rs` cover flat deposition, independent f64 sloped erosion in all four classes, repeatability, erosion after deposition, moving tiles, scratch/ledger isolation, dry/unrepresentable exchanges, 20/40/80 Hz erosion under constant forcing, and 20,000 alternating stages per class at nonbinary water depth. The repeated-budget tolerance is fixed at one part per million of initial mobile material, excluding background bed volume. The measured maximum residual is 1.084 mL, or 0.565 ppm. These isolated controls represent 1,000 seconds of prescribed alternating forcing per class; they are not a constructed pond or full-site duration test.

The initial 1024 m layer stress fixture exposed 3.750 L maximum error in the old requested-transfer path. The same fixture now reports zero residual at its measured precision. At ordinary layer thicknesses, the independent sloped erosion reference differs by at most 4.142e-9 m. Constant-forcing erosion differs from its f64 solution by at most 2.822e-7 m over the three tested timesteps. These bounds apply to the controls and forcing described in the test.

Physical limitations remain explicit. The inherited settling law ignores deposits below 1 mm or concentration changes below 1e-6 in each step, adds a calm-water boost per step, and uses a frame-count stability bonus. Dilute fines can remain suspended indefinitely; general deposition timestep convergence is unproven. `sluice-terrain-settling` tracks removing those artefacts with independent dilute/quiescent/residence-time controls. The [HEC deposition formulation](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/d2sd/ras2dsedtr/6.6/model-description/deposition) relates deposition flux to settling velocity and concentration, with adaptation or deposition-probability terms. A fixed-depth, well-mixed exponential decay can be derived from that flux as a control case; it is not a claim that the present model implements HEC or dense-slurry behaviour.

The terrain inventory still uses the existing layer-volume convention. Conserved grain mass, porosity, pore water, compaction, hindered settling, flocculation, local trace-gold transport and empirical erosion/settling calibration remain separate requirements in the full operation plan. FLIP/APIC, DEM, riffles, particle counts, pressure iterations, water timestep and terrain resolution are unchanged by this numerical correction.
