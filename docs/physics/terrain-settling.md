Terrain sediment now deposits continuously with physical time and can scour through a thin cover into the next exposed stratum. This removes the former 1 mm deposit gate, concentration gate, per-tick calm-water boost and per-tick fraction cap. It changes the environmental bed model; the sluice's FLIP/APIC, DEM and riffle solver are unchanged.

For each noncohesive size class, the model assumes a vertically well-mixed water column and uses deposition flux `D = ws*C`. With fixed water depth `h`, this gives `dC/dt = -ws*C/h` and the integrated transfer `h*C*(1-exp(-ws*dt/h))`. Small exponents use a cancellation-safe polynomial. The existing four median diameters, densities, viscosity and Stokes/turbulent terminal-velocity blend are retained. The assumption corresponds to an adaptation coefficient of one in the [HEC deposition formulation](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/d2sd/ras2dsedtr/6.6/model-description/deposition); it does not implement the full HEC model or a calibrated slurry law.

Deposition and scour operate in the same step. Scour retains the existing Shields threshold, hardness and erosion-rate cap. It spends time removing the actual exposed layer, then uses the remaining time on the next layer. A 20 micrometre sediment cover therefore consumes its removal time rather than protecting the underlying soil for a whole tick. A stale visual surface-material tag cannot select physical exposure. Bedrock remains non-erodible. The former 5 cm/s erosion-speed floor remains a model limitation.

Each stage still snapshots unchanged geometry, writes a candidate bed and reconciles against stored f32 values in a separate pass. Complete deposition now steps an overdrawn candidate down by representable increments instead of rejecting the whole load. Nearby value differences are anchored through their binary mantissas; fused residuals refine division and compare neighbouring representable recipients. A small deposit may align with the representable concentration transfer when this reduces error. Full scour clears stay exact, avoiding an artificial residual film that could shield the next stratum.

Independent rounding at every exchange still produced systematic drift, so each cell now retains four signed rounding-feedback values. The next exchange corrects that prior error and records the newly observed remainder. These are numerical compensation, not an extra material inventory: the unchanged budget checks count actual bed, suspension and exports. The buffer costs 16 bytes per cell, or 256 KiB at 128×128, plus the same additional readback payload per terrain tick. It adds one storage binding and no simulation dispatches. This is measured finite-precision conservation, not an exact-arithmetic guarantee.

The stability clock now stores elapsed microseconds, capped at 1.5 seconds, rather than counting calls. This preserves the old 30-tick curve at the playable game's 20 Hz terrain rate. Still wet beds can age; zero elapsed time cannot age or exchange material. Accepted bed changes reset the clock at their stage boundary. The existing maximum 50% threshold bonus remains an uncalibrated stability proxy, not soil consolidation, armouring or cohesive strength.

New saves use version 3 with an explicit clock unit and rounding-feedback state. Version 2 saves without the unit convert old ticks once at 50,000 microseconds per tick and start with zero feedback. Loading validates the version/unit pairing and quantities before migration. Resaving preserves physical age. Live GPU upload/readback and restore preserve both fields. Old version-2-only builds reject the new version rather than interpreting microseconds as tick counts.

Seventeen isolated real-GPU controls cover the four size classes, three water depths, dilute submillimetre deposits, zero time, long residence, thin-cover washout, bed age, post-deposition slopes, moving tiles, independent f64 recipient balances and repeated exchange. The original coupled FLIP/DEM restore and boundary inventories also run. The long repeated controls retain their one-ppm limit relative to initial mobile material, excluding background bed. Measured final residuals after 20,000 stages per class are at most 0.0964 mL, or 0.0503 ppm. A long coarse-settling case leaves only 4.48e-8 of its initial mobile load; before the full-transfer correction, 65.13% remained suspended.

The dilute-column maximum concentration error is 1.11e-6 relative to its initial concentration. Coupled settling/scour errors decrease from 6.46e-6 to 3.31e-6 to 1.50e-6 as the timestep halves, against an independently integrated well-mixed balance. These are numerical controls under prescribed conditions, not laboratory calibration or a constructed settling-pond trial.

Important remaining limits are unchanged: exchange is eligible only at water depths of at least 1 mm; drying and rewetting sediment-bearing films need separate treatment. Visual material classification still has a 1 mm thickness threshold. Layer quantities retain the existing volume convention; grain mass, porosity, pore water, compaction, hindered settling, flocculation, erosion calibration and substep-local trace-gold transport remain open. The [full operation plan](../../plans/2026-10-22-realistic-operation.md) still requires constructed pads, ponds, shaker decks, phase coupling and measured live operation. Passing these controls does not complete those stages.
