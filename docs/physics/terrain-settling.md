Terrain sediment deposits continuously with physical time at every positive water depth. It can also scour through a thin cover into the next exposed stratum when the existing erosion model applies. The sluice's FLIP/APIC, DEM and riffle solver are unchanged.

For each noncohesive size class, the model assumes a vertically well-mixed column and uses deposition flux `D = ws*C`. At fixed water depth `h`, this gives `dC/dt = -ws*C/h` and `C_next = C_old*exp(-ws*dt/h)`. The original four median diameters, densities, viscosity and Stokes/turbulent terminal-velocity blend are retained. An adaptation coefficient of one corresponds to the [HEC deposition formulation](https://www.hec.usace.army.mil/confluence/rasdocs/d2sd/ras2dsedtr/6.6/model-description/deposition); this is not the full HEC model or a calibrated slurry law.

Deposition chooses the representable new concentration and transfers its removed material into the counted [two-part bed](subgrid-bed.md). The positive subgeometry remainder is actual inventory, rather than discarded sediment or a numerical error budget. This removes the old 1 mm water-depth eligibility floor and an unphysical suspended tail caused by an unreceivable geometric increment. Integer-anchored, magnitude-ordered residuals preserve the product and sum corrections on the real GPU.

Conservative water/material advection retains its CFL substeps. Bed exchange then operates over the complete terrain tick. Settling uses an immutable snapshot and one exchange pass; scour retains the separately stored candidate and reconciliation passes. A stationary cell with unchanged depth and zero face transport preserves concentration exactly. It no longer repeatedly rounds `C*h/h` while no material moves.

Scour retains the existing Shields threshold, hardness, erosion-rate cap, 1 mm water-depth applicability floor and 5 cm/s speed floor. It spends physical time removing the actual exposed stratum before using remaining time on the next one. Coarse visual surface-material tags do not select that exposure, and bedrock remains non-erodible. The existing signed exchange-feedback values compensate scour roundoff; they are separate from counted bed inventory. Under-1-mm rough-bed entrainment needs further physical treatment.

The stability clock stores elapsed microseconds, capped at 1.5 seconds. Accepted bed changes reset it; still wet beds can age in the applicable scour stage, and zero elapsed time cannot age or exchange material. The maximum 50 percent threshold bonus remains an uncalibrated stability proxy, rather than soil consolidation, armouring or cohesive strength.

Current version 5 saves preserve the new bed material. Clock/feedback state was introduced in version 3 and finite moisture in version 4. Version 2 legacy tick ages convert once at 50,000 microseconds per tick. Versions 2–4 load without invented new stock; incompatible version/state combinations and invalid quantities are rejected. Readback, excavation, deposition and live restore retain canonical inventory.

The current isolated GPU controls include seven water depths on both sides of 1 mm, three uniform cell sizes and four timesteps, alongside source-precision, zero-time, long-residence, thin-cover washout, bed-age, post-deposition-slope, moving-tile and repeated-exchange controls. The maximum uniform-column concentration error is 0.922 ppm of the initial concentration and material error is 0.135 ppm of initial mobile material. These uniform controls do not establish sloping wet/dry shoreline convergence.

After 20,000 settling/scour stages per class, measured final material residuals are below 0.042 ppm of initial mobile material, with the existing one-ppm limit unchanged and background bed excluded. Coupled concentration errors decrease from 6.44e-6 to 3.31e-6 to 1.50e-6 as the timestep halves against an independently integrated mixed-column reference. Prescribed drying retains deposited classes; quiet rewetting leaves them on the bed, and the existing deeper-flow scour re-entrains them within the same numerical budget. Those controls prescribe water levels; they are not an infiltration or evaporation law.

Canonical grain-mass conversion and finite wet-material custody are implemented. Remaining requirements include continuous drying/rewetting, sloping pond margins, variable porosity, dynamic pore flow, compaction, hindered settling, flocculation, physical erosion calibration, arbitrary deposited strata and substep-local trace-gold transport. Visual material classification still uses a 1 mm layer threshold. The [full operation plan](../../plans/2026-10-22-realistic-operation.md) still requires constructed pads, ponds, shaker decks, phase coupling and measured live operation. These numerical controls do not complete those stages.
