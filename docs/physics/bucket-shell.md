New articulated machines use a hollow backhoe bucket shared by collision and rendering. Ordinary shifts retain their existing finite cutting model, and previously saved articulated machines retain their `LegacyFloor` model. The new `ShellV1` is a declared generic design, not manufacturer calibration.

The bucket has an 820 mm outer width, a 280 litre internal cavity, a curved floor/back, two cheek plates and five tapered teeth. Fifteen convex steel components form one compound collider. Each visual component uses the exact f32 hull vertices and outward closed faces used for collision. The open mouth remains empty; a convex hull over the entire bucket would incorrectly seal it. The tooth roots meet the internal floor, rather than forming the 60 mm raised entry obstruction reproduced in the first bank replay.

Uniform steel density is declared as 7,850 kg/m³. A bounded solve chooses plate thickness for a 250 kg integrated steel mass and adjusts the profile height to retain 280 litres of internal volume. Signed closed-triangle volume integrals determine steel COM and the full central inertia, including its cross term. Each collider has zero density; the body receives the integrated mass properties explicitly so mass is counted once. The existing explicit carried-payload API adds its actual spatial moments. Its uniform fallback clips the real cavity at a local fill level; it does not distribute new payload over the old floor proxy.

The wrist mount faces the bucket toward the machine. Shell orientation is `upper * Rx(-(boom + stick - curl + pi/2)) * Ry(pi)`. The fourth hinge uses `+curl`; old machines retain the previous mount, chart and arithmetic. The minimum local floor point and actual tooth tip drive the tool diagnostics. Registration tests independently compare every hull vertex, COM, mass and rotated inertia with the mesh frame at multiple tilted poses and payloads.

Active soil remains independent finite bodies in the same solve as the links, tracks, substrate and equipment. There is no capture box, suction, material injection, prescribed tool pose or disabled soil contact. The HUD's bucket estimate classifies clod centres inside the nominal cavity only for observation; it never transfers custody or adds the clods to carried scalar mass. Bodies crossing the mouth can be partly inside, so this is an approximate load readout.

The prepared dry-bank replay supplies ordinary joystick axes: crowd, curl with grade correction, lift, hold and open. The grade controller responds to the actual lowest steel clearance over the lowered solid substrate. It is an opt-in validation controller, not a digging law. The initial bank is withdrawn from finite site stock before time zero. Subsequent steps change no inventories except through the existing physical evolution. The first flush-entry replay lifts 4.8 litres, retains that load above the bank and empties through the mouth. This small yield proves a contact-mediated cycle; it does not establish realistic productivity or calibrated bulk soil response.

Version 10 stores `ShellV1` explicitly. The loader rejects a shell under older format tags and rejects version 10 without its shell model. Versions 2–9 keep their previous migrations, and old articulated machines retain their floor proxy and old save version. No new hidden dynamic state is introduced; cached geometry is immutable and contact worlds still rebuild at each mechanical boundary.

Static gravity tests retain eight clods upright and at a 45 degree roll at both 240 and 480 Hz, and discharge all eight through an inverted mouth. The floor-only negative control loses the rolled load. Separate free cheek/floor impacts require machine recoil, closed linear and angular momentum, and no invented energy. The real-bank regression requires a meaningful load held wholly clear of the terrain and complete emptying after opening, with every material channel conserved and scalar carried custody empty. These checks do not prove continuum soil convergence, wet mud, cohesive failure, efficient digging or full excavator calibration. Broader Stages 2–6 of the [operation plan](../../plans/2026-10-22-realistic-operation.md) remain open.

Native review also exposes a remaining engineering defect: the generic stick end enters the open cavity. Shared shell registration does not validate that mounting arrangement. The [checkpoint evidence](../playtests/2026-10-23-bucket-shell.md) records the intrusive mount, the strongly timestep-sensitive bank yield and failed real-time gates. The shell stage remains in progress pending exterior attachment and bulk convergence; no calibrated digging or fully realistic bucket claim is made.
