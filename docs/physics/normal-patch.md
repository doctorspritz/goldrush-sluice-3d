# Dimensional normal material patch: objective forces and stored energy

This Stage 2 component defines a normal elastic/viscous material interface independently of body mass, collision-point count and solver frequency. It is a library model for calibration and subsequent coupling. It is **not coupled to live soil**, assigns no real-soil default values and changes no Session, rendering or save policy. The existing bank's energy failures remain unresolved.

The admitted geometry is a finite convex reference material patch on body A against an unbounded plane attached to body B. Reference patch area stays fixed under rigid rotation; it is not the changing projection onto the opposing plane. The model is not a finite-face collision law, a Hertz grain model or a bulk soil continuum. Admitted linear-layer parameters are a specified constrained normal modulus M in Pa, reference thickness L in metres, and viscosity in Pa s. The pressure/displacement slope is M/L in N/m³; uniform force stiffness is M·area/L in N/m. No body mass or contact effective mass rescales these coefficients.

Linear normal pressure/overclosure stiffness is a standard contact-model form ([Abaqus enforcement documentation](https://docs.software.vt.edu/abaqusv2025/English/SIMACAEITNRefMap/simaitn-c-expcontactconstraints.htm)); dimensional Hooke stiffness and the option to exclude attractive damping are documented separately in [LAMMPS](https://docs.lammps.org/pair_granular.html). The reference-area layer model and its integration below are our explicitly specified model, not an inference that either reference calibrates our soil. M is a constrained normal modulus, not silently substituted for Young's modulus.

At each actual relative pose, gap g and normal relative velocity v are affine over each material triangle. For g≤0, pressure is p=max(0,-(M/L)g-(viscosity/L)v); open material has zero traction. Stored elastic energy is U=½(M/L)∫min(g,0)² dA. The current body-B normal and both bodies' angular velocities determine v. This avoids freezing a world normal or treating common rigid rotation as compression rate.

Two exact half-plane clips select geometric compression and positive traction. Closed-form linear/quadratic triangle moments integrate force, center-of-pressure moment, stored energy and work rate, including partial contact and unloading. Resultant forces are equal and opposite; torques are paired about each current center of mass. For pure elasticity they are the negative pose derivative of the same U. With viscosity, instantaneous body power + U rate + dissipation closes, including opening regions whose tensile dashpot traction is capped. Raw signed floating-point residuals remain visible; no favourable strain credit or negative-dissipation clamp changes a gate.

Using one independent spring per corner is insufficient even after dividing total stiffness by the point count. In a uniformly compressed rectangular plate with a small tilt, the corner-spring control has exactly **three times** the correct rotational stiffness: it replaces the patch's second area moment by its corner moment. The integrated model retains both the uniform load stiffness and the correct moment. This is why the new component uses material area and its moments rather than equal collision-point springs.

Additional analytic controls retain uniform pressure, force/displacement stiffness, known load support, elastic energy, a partially compressed rotating rectangle, common rigid pose/velocity covariance, and total force/moment balance. The refinement control changes the partition of the **same material domain**; it does not establish soil-particle resolution convergence or loaded excavation accuracy. Common pose and motion are transformed together, including an added common translation and angular velocity. These are controls of this new normal model; the legacy solver's tangential-frame/history issues remain open.
