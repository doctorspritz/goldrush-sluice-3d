# Coupled dynamics for the normal material patch

The dimensional contact layer now drives translation and rotation of two bodies together. This is a calibration component in `soil_response::dynamics`, advancing specified material, mass and principal inertia. It does not select a Session policy or replace the retained soil solver. Finite opposing faces, contact ownership/activation, joints, friction, yielding and wet soil remain required before live integration.

`step_pair` solves up to twelve coupled midpoint velocity unknowns with bounded Newton iterations and line search. Each body retains its principal inertia and world orientation; free anisotropic rotation conserves the direction of world angular momentum as well as kinetic energy. Positions and Cayley rotations advance from the same accepted unknowns. Failed input, rotation admission, quadrature, convergence or work closure returns no advanced state. No velocity, impulse, pose or energy correction follows the solve.

For each material point, elastic pressure is the negative secant of the same compression potential used by the instantaneous component. Capped Kelvin–Voigt traction is integrated over the active portion of a linear endpoint-gap path. Averaging first and capping afterwards is wrong during unloading: the independent opening fixture gives 2.901315789 Pa, versus 1 Pa from that naive method, and 0.144375 J/m² dissipation.

Endpoint-averaged normals and material arms form the discrete force/torque pair; the averaged normal is deliberately not renormalized. They pair with the Cayley angular increment and exact endpoint-gap change. Applied torque work uses that discrete increment, rather than claiming an exact continuous finite-angle work integral. The method's continuous-motion accuracy must be established by physical timestep refinement.

Five affine partition lines select compression, entry/exit and capped-traction regions. Material elastic work, contact work and damping loss are polynomial within each partition; their root quadrature is retained while force and moment refine adaptively. Refinement uses a global force/moment error budget and a positive, symmetric degree-five triangle rule from [Dunavant (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1620210612), independently checked against every triangle monomial through degree five. The algebraic coefficients were cross-checked against the [Drake implementation](https://github.com/RobotLocomotion/drake/blob/master/multibody/triangle_quadrature/gaussian_triangle_quadrature_rule.h). The adaptive estimate is numerical evidence, not a rigorous physical-error bound.

The initial area-proportional budgets refused rotating partial contact at an absolute contribution of 5.46e-16 N. Global allocation fixes that failure without widening the configured patch target. The lower-order rule then exceeded the unchanged 300,000-sample cap at the tightest independent rational integral. The higher-order rule completes it with 85,638 samples; actual combined force/moment error is 5.76e-8 N, against a 3.01e-6 N estimated error. This is a component evaluation count, not a native performance result. Duplicate zero-gap clipping vertices were separately reproduced (four slots for three unique vertices) and corrected without shifting a boundary.

Each accepted step reports kinetic, gravitational and stored elastic energy; applied work; non-tensile damping loss; material/contact work residuals; linear/angular impulse residuals; and accepted endpoint quadrature cost. The default absolute work-closure admission is 1e-8 J. Residuals remain raw; a failed audit rejects the step rather than changing state or crediting strain to the old solver. Accepted quadrature samples do not include all Newton trial cost.

Fourteen new controls cover loaded support, independent oscillator accuracy, elastic release/opening, two moving anisotropic bodies, free rotation, applied loads, common world pose and Galilean drift, material-domain refinement, rotating-contact timestep refinement, capped unloading, rational force integration, polynomial quadrature, clipping and atomic refusal.

The rotating error metric is position error plus T times linear-velocity error plus 0.5 m times T times angular-velocity error, with T = 1/30 s, against a 15,360 Hz numerical reference. It is not an analytic excavation result. Analytic oscillator controls at 100 and 1,000 kg improve by approximately four when timestep halves, with identical material coefficients. The 100 kg control's normalized trajectory error is still 13.99% at 480 Hz, 3.51% at 960 Hz and 0.876% at 1,920 Hz. Energy preservation alone is not sufficient accuracy, and these values are not adopted real-soil parameters.

The admitted geometry remains a finite reference patch against a body-attached unbounded plane. No arbitrary clod or terrain pair may receive this potential as accounting credit without matching material forces, finite ownership/domain admission and a consistently coupled solve. Dense two-body Newton calibration is not a scalable whole-bank algorithm or continuous collision detection. Continuous digging, loaded retention, wet/cohesive mud, pads, shaker decks, sluice buildup/scour, settling/recirculation, gameplay/art and physical real time remain open.
