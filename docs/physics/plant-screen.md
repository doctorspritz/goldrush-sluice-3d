# First integrated screen circuit

The saved circuit connects a finite existing hopper to a mechanically moving wire screen, an undersize receiving gutter, the original wet sluice and an oversize catch bin. Stable typed ports admit only this first fixed circuit. Arbitrary module routing, a trommel and complete wet processing remain required.

The model is a dilute one-way population of weighted physical-diameter spheres. Each parcel retains its mineral and attached pore-water inventory, physical diameter, velocity, spin, rotation and affine state. Its weight represents mass; it does not become a large collision ball. Position, velocity and spin are authoritative double-precision state while screen-owned; the existing Grain float mirrors serve rendering and the existing handoff boundary, where float representation error remains an inherited limit. Material tags do not select screen destinations. Geometric passage between capsule wires, contact with gutter triangles and actual bin-floor contact determine outputs. The rendering uses those same segments and triangles, with saved-phase interpolation rather than a decorative oscillator.

The drive applies prescribed translation. Continuous casts over bounded kick-drift-kick intervals cover particle and wire motion. Inflated chord envelopes only bracket possible contacts; exact-phase gap refinement must qualify actual reaction. Open neighbors do not become friction supports merely because they lie within the curvature envelope. The acceleration-based chord bound, work/contact/event/storage limits and exact endpoint clearance bound are numerical admission checks; exhaustion retains owned state and pauses with a diagnostic. They are not physical clogging or capacity laws. An explicit saved policy uses the configured restitution for isolated supports and fully plastic normal response for simultaneous supports. This applies consistently, not only after a solver refusal. It avoids incompatible mixed per-contact Newton rebound targets, but omits elastic strain/preload and simultaneous rebound; those remain required under `menace-sfu.2.1`. A transient spring impact is not implemented: a self-locking wedge can retain elastic energy and requires persistent strain ownership rather than a fictitious loss. A bounded coupled solve accounts for simultaneous wire contacts: exact active-set normal projection and bounded contact solves. Two simultaneous supports first check double sticking. A bounded exact-Jacobian proposal may then certify the same original complementarity/Coulomb equations before the fallback nine open/stick/slide combinations. A missing derivative can use the original projected-map descent direction; exhausting a proposal does not change geometry, friction, restitution or admission tolerances. At fixed positive sliding multipliers, the two-contact solver eliminates tangent impulses through the normal Schur response, then solves at most two cone equations in log multipliers with exact derivatives and a bounded dimensionless seed ladder. Compatible double sticking also checks the one-dimensional internal-force interval against both cones. Configured friction remains fixed; no trial friction continuation enters this method. Every final candidate must satisfy the declared unilateral/Coulomb equations and unchanged energy gate; other support counts use the projected-map solve with exact active-normal and smooth-disk derivatives, followed by a kinetic-energy versus actual actuator-work check. Trial iterations do not mutate the body. Configured friction, geometric limits and work/event budgets are not relaxed. The simultaneous impact law is explicitly plastic; it is not claimed to preserve the earlier inconsistent per-contact restitution equations. Surface-relative normal and Coulomb impulses account for actual motor work. The internal friction branch enumeration is a numerical method for the specified sphere contact model, not evidence of empirical rock/clay behavior. Original FLIP/APIC water, riffles, granular wet-sluice equations and terrain water policies remain unchanged.

Screen output retains full emitted Grain state through the receiver transform. Grain moisture becomes actual sluice feed-water credit only when accepted. Receiver storage is reserved before donor removal. Products wait until the start of a fresh two-tick sluice interval, adding up to two fixed ticks of queue delay; exact fractional-age domain continuation is not implemented. Numerical refusal retains the donor. Spills retain their actual world state in the existing tailings owner.

The bin holds post-contact product records. It does not resolve ongoing dense settling or a rising packed bed. The deck has no parcel-parcel collisions, cohesion, clay adhesion, packing, bridging, screen blinding or coupled spray/film water. Attached moisture affects wet particle mass and is conserved; that does not establish wet-screen hydraulics or realistic slurry rheology. Neither empirical screen throughput nor gold recovery is calibrated.

K fits/starts/stops the screen near the plant, M changes aperture after shutdown and emptying, and L explicitly removes all stopped deck/product stock and held water into the existing bulk hand inventory. These maintenance controls also operate while paused at the plant. This is manual clearing, not simulated aperture passage or bonus recovery. Existing bulk hand/haul/dump/intake/hopper boundaries still discard grading/history; the screen-to-sluice handoff itself avoids resampling. Finite geological pay must first be exposed beneath actual overburden.

Version-14 saves own the whole circuit and require its connected sluice, mineral tailings and independent world gold owners. Downgraded envelopes cannot hide its material. Legacy version-13 operations preserve their old circuit until the player fits a screen. The demo starts with a running screen and the existing warmed wet sluice.

The final operation still requires dense/wet deck physics, both shaker/trommel types, upgrades and placement, actual wet-feed plugs/blinding, tailings obstruction/instability, ponds/recirculation, haulage, surveys, wet access/dewatering, bogging, slumps/deep-cut collapse, malfunctions/repair and flood/berm/bridge failures. One dry-screen checkpoint does not complete that scope.

Rigid contact is itself an approximation. The isolated velocity restitution, plastic simultaneous response and prescribed motor do not establish real rock/wire mechanics. [Uchida, Sherman and Delp (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4984984/) discuss why simultaneous frictional impacts require care, including energy consistency and changes in sliding direction. This implementation is not their PLUS impact model; compliant/empirical screen calibration remains future work.

For the plastic policy, passivity follows from the actual boundary work: ΔK − W = Σ Jᵢ · cᵢ(after) − ½ Δqᵀ M Δq ≤ 0. Normal complementarity makes the normal term zero, and endpoint Coulomb impulses have nonpositive tangential dot product. This is local plastic Coulomb complementarity, not a claimed global maximum-dissipation algorithm or proof of unique impulses. The stored double-spin candidate is the state covered by the energy gate.

Static internal impulses can be indeterminate and vary with the tangent rank basis even when the tested body motion and total actuator work agree. These impulses are not calibrated wire loads and must not be reused as a wear/failure model without physical load-sharing qualification.

For three or more common-velocity supports, zero-impulse feasibility takes precedence. A bounded geometric pair/third construction may then certify a fully static body in that common frame; zero relative kinetic energy is the greatest possible relative energy loss. It preserves its explicit normal-force witness and checks the same nonnegative impulse, normal complementarity, Coulomb and work/energy conditions. It does not recover a different sparse normal distribution. If no such static certificate is available, the exact-derivative projected-map solve remains bounded. This is a finite candidate selection, not a globally complete cone optimizer.

CPU profiling exposed excessive repeated contact search and allocation. Four-dimensional reduced elimination now uses stack arrays with unchanged complete pivot order and rank thresholds; a 512-case equivalence control compares its results exactly with the dynamic version. Line searches omit unused derivatives. A feasible zero normal correction returns immediately because no correction can have smaller norm. The transaction caches immutable drive basis/geometry, with exact cached/uncached controls and no serialized cache. Swept-sphere bounding boxes reject only spatially disjoint static triangles before the unchanged narrowphase. The physical intervals, exact-phase refinement, contact skin, configured friction/restitution and final energy/budget gates remain unchanged. Numerical root proposals can select another admitted contact solution where the rigid law is nonunique; no whole-trajectory bitwise equivalence to the earlier seed search is claimed.
