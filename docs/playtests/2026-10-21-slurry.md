# Dense slurry representation and local suspension

New shifts separate 20–200 micrometre noncohesive soil fines from 0.2–1.2 mm sand. Physical grading feeds the existing size-dependent drag/settling law. All material components remain exact; water/riffle resolution, pressure sweeps, feed volume and the contact budget are preserved. Old saves keep their previous grading. The [model notes](../physics/slurry.md) describe assumptions and outstanding physics work.

## What changed visibly

The whole-channel dirt tint is replaced by local 3D suspension optics derived from actual moving fine-parcel volume, grain cross section and water volume. Fine plumes move with the saved material; settled dirt does not make all water cloudy. A continuous read-only reconstruction avoids discrete coloured surface tiles. The existing outlet flight and terrain/river surface are retained.

The grain sampler now uses the existing 32,768-point budget more effectively, with physical-size samples and individual gold preserved. Three material batches use exact-sized mesh allocations and skip subpixel grain shadows. No extra simulated material or independent collisions are introduced by display sampling.

## Native verification

On the exact same frozen previous save/camera, visible grain samples increased from 7,216 to 32,590. Mean FPS measured 57.72 before and 56.43 after. Water particle count, airbornes, simulation time and water/gold residuals match exactly. Background load changed between checks, so these measurements are not a controlled speedup/regression estimate.

The final live graded demo averaged **45.74 FPS and 0.6673x physical speed** over 20.0060 seconds after five seconds of warmup. Water residual was -9.815 mL and gold residual 5.46e-12 g. The real-time gate returned **failure**. Physical throughput and rendering FPS remain separate metrics.

The earlier 65,536-proxy trial measured 41.87 FPS; it was superseded by restoring the original global display budget and eliminating repeated buffer growth. Its raw receipts and images are preserved separately. Final frozen and live captures were inspected from the signed app, including dirty channel water, the outlet and the visible river bank. These captures do not establish calibrated slurry dynamics.

All **74** focused tests pass: 33 domain, 15 physics with real GPU checks explicitly included, and 26 app tests. They cover per-component feed conservation, grading/save compatibility, local and edge projection, grain-size effects, parcel subdivision, settled-water clearing, bounded dense geometry, individual gold and the existing complete water/riffle/terrain pipeline with live restore. Strict focused Clippy passes; the game dependency retains its existing unused-constructor warning. The final demo asset SHA-256 is `9069242826d9e8091f19ccbfb33bd0ff5a0cd41db3049b04097b72e75c5705ff`.

## Physical limits

This improves the declared size distribution and visible representation. Weighted fine parcels still share one coarse trajectory/contact envelope. The closed phase-momentum budget, packed-bed contacts, porosity-aware equations, clay cohesion/rheology and experimental grading/recovery calibration remain open. Airborne water uses the current local outlet extinction for presentation; this is not an independently transported airborne sediment-mass ledger. See the [accuracy gates](../physics/accuracy.md) and the [numeric receipts](2026-10-21-slurry.json).
